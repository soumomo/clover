% ==============================================================================
\noindent\textbf{\large 1. Technical Approach \& Core Philosophy}\\[0.25em]
High-integrity Measurement, Reporting, and Verification (MRV) in nature-based carbon markets requires auditable, physically grounded tree data. Traditional forestry relies on manual field sampling (measuring $1\text{--}2\%$ of trees with diameter tapes), which is logistically impossible across large landscapes. Conversely, commercial satellite AI platforms routinely overpromise: they draw rectangular bounding boxes around green pixels, sum the areas, and output carbon credits with unscientific zero-error certainty. When audited by carbon registries (Verra, Gold Standard), these inflated claims are rejected.

\textbf{Clover} was built strictly on Flora Carbon's founding principle: \textit{``A rough tool that admits what it can't do is more valuable than a polished one that invents numbers.''} Instead of treating AI as a black box, Clover enforces sensor physics gating, converts bounding boxes into true crown ellipses, topologically dissolves overlapping canopies so foliage is never double-counted, applies empirical sensor calibration from peer-reviewed ground LiDAR, and propagates honest statistical uncertainty through allometric carbon models.

% SECTION 2: ARCHITECTURE & END-TO-END WORKFLOW
% ==============================================================================
\noindent\textbf{\large 2. Architecture \& End-to-End Workflow}\\[0.25em]
Clover executes a six-stage, auditable geospatial micro-pipeline implemented in Python (\texttt{canopy\_core}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Image Sharpness Check (GSD Physics Gating)}: Before running inference, Clover inspects raster affine metadata to calculate metric Ground Sampling Distance (GSD). Detection is tiered: Tier 1 ($<0.15\,\text{m}$, drone), Tier 2 ($0.15\text{--}0.50\,\text{m}$, sub-meter satellite), and Tier 3 ($0.50\text{--}1.0\,\text{m}$, emergent trees). If an uploaded raster exceeds $1.0\,\text{m}$ (e.g., $10\,\text{m}$ Sentinel-2), an entire tree is smaller than a single pixel. Clover rejects coarse rasters with an explicit warning rather than hallucinating fake tree counts.
    \item \textbf{Metric Coordinate Reprojection}: Raw satellite rasters use geographic coordinates (WGS84 degrees), which distort distances based on latitude ($\cos \phi$). Clover automatically reprojects rasters into local metric coordinate systems (e.g., UTM Zone 45N, EPSG:32645 for Kolkata), guaranteeing that $1\,\text{pixel} \times 1\,\text{pixel}$ strictly represents Euclidean square meters ($1\,\text{m}^2$) on the ground.
    \item \textbf{Sliding-Window Slicing with 25\% Overlap Halo}: Gigapixel mosaics ($10,000 \times 10,000\,\text{px}$) crash GPU memory if ingested whole. Clover tiles rasters into $400 \times 400\,\text{px}$ chips with a $25\%$ overlap halo ($100\,\text{px}$ border margin, stride = $300\,\text{px}$). This guarantees that tree crowns growing across tile boundary seams are never split or missed.
    \item \textbf{Neural Crown Detection (DeepForest RetinaNet)}: Runs a ResNet-50 Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) pretrained on 30+ million crowns. The model uses Focal Loss ($\gamma=2.0, \alpha=0.25$) to handle severe foreground-background class imbalance where $>95\%$ of pixels are background soil, grass, or buildings.
    \item \textbf{Global Boundary Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS)}: Predictions from overlapping tiles are projected back to real-world metric coordinates. Clover applies boundary NMS ($\text{IoU} = 0.30$) to deduplicate seam detections, retaining only the single highest-confidence detection per tree.
    \item \textbf{Automated Hydrological Weed Filter}: Floating green mats of invasive water hyacinth (\textit{Eichhornia crassipes}) in urban lakes share foliar color with trees. Clover isolates waterbodies via color-ratio masking, preventing aquatic weed mats from being falsely counted as forest canopy.
\end{enumerate}

% SECTION 3: KEY TECHNICAL DECISIONS: COMPUTATIONAL GEOMETRY
% ==============================================================================
\noindent\textbf{\large 3. Key Technical Decisions: Computational Geometry of Canopy Cover}\\[0.25em]
A core failure of existing tree tools is treating bounding boxes as canopy area. Clover replaces naive box addition with three rigorous computational geometry steps:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Circumscribed Ellipse Correction ($\pi/4$)}: Object detection models output rectangles of area $A_{\text{box}} = W \times H$. However, natural tree crowns viewed from above are circular or elliptical. The 4 empty corners of any bounding box contain bare ground, turf, or pavement. Naively summing boxes introduces an immediate, deterministic $\mathbf{+21.46\%}$ overestimation error ($1 - \pi/4 \approx 0.2146$). Clover analytically fits circumscribed Shapely ellipses ($A_{\text{ellipse}} = \frac{\pi}{4} W H \approx 0.7854 \cdot A_{\text{box}}$) inside every detected box, mathematically removing non-tree background.
    \item \textbf{Topological Unary Union Dissolution ($\bigcup \mathcal{P}_i$)}: In closed stands, campus groves, and natural forests, branches intertwine. Simply adding individual crown areas $\sum A_i$ counts shared foliage twice, routinely producing impossible canopy covers ($>100\%$). Clover runs GEOS spatial sweep-line dissolution to merge overlapping crown ellipses into a single continuous polygon footprint:
    \begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{C}_{\text{dissolved}} = \bigcup_{i=1}^N \mathcal{P}_i \implies \text{Canopy Cover \%} = \frac{\text{Area}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{dissolved}} \cap \mathcal{A}_{\text{AOI}})}{\text{Area}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{AOI}})} \times 100\%
    \end{equation*}
    \item \textbf{Capturing Faded Branch Tips (\href{https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad076}{Li et al., PNAS Nexus 2023})}: Optical RGB sensors clearly capture the dense, sunlit core of a crown, but miss semi-translucent outer twigs and sun-bleached branch tips. Ground LiDAR comparisons by Li et al. (2023) established that 2D optical models underestimate real crown projection by $\approx 20\%$. Clover applies this $+20\%$ calibration to report both raw optical and ground-calibrated canopy footprints.
\end{itemize}

% ==============================================================================
\noindent\textbf{\large 4. Allometry \& Dual-Component Uncertainty (Why Zero-Error Claims are Fraudulent)}\\[0.25em]
Satellites see trees from above — they cannot measure trunk diameter ($DBH$) or wood density ($\rho$) hidden beneath foliage. We estimate biomass using crown diameter ($CD = \sqrt{W \cdot H}$) power-law models grounded in destructive field harvests:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\href{https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.13388}{Jucker et al. (2016)}} Pantropical Biomass: Calibrated on 2,000+ destructively weighed trees across 4 continents: $\ln(\text{AGB}_i) = \alpha + \beta \ln(CD_i) \implies \text{AGB}_i = \exp\left(-0.328 + 2.404 \ln(CD_i)\right)$ [kg dry wood]. Stand biomass is $\text{AGB}_{\text{stand}} = \frac{1}{1000} \sum_{i=1}^N \text{AGB}_i$ [Mg].
    \item \textbf{Carbon Stock \& $\text{CO}_{2\text{e}}$}: Dry biomass is $47\%$ elemental carbon (IPCC standard): $C_{\text{stock}} = \text{AGB}_{\text{stand}} \times 0.47$. Multiplying by the molecular weight ratio ($44/12 \approx 3.664$) yields equivalent atmospheric $\text{CO}_{2\text{e}}$ sequestered.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Component Uncertainty Formulation}: Estimating weight from crown width alone carries single-tree random variance ($\sigma_{\text{tree}} \approx \pm 56.5\%$). Under the \textbf{Central Limit Theorem (CLT)}, random errors cancel out across $N$ trees: $\sigma_{\text{stat}} = 56.5\% / \sqrt{N}$. Crucially, pantropical models have an irreducible \textbf{systematic error floor of $\pm 12.0\%$} (\href{https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.12629}{Chave et al., 2014}; \href{https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12753}{R\'ejou-M\'echain et al., 2017}) due to regional wood density and taper variations that *never* average away: $\sigma_{\text{total}} = \sqrt{\sigma_{\text{stat}}^2 + \sigma_{\text{sys}}^2} = \sqrt{(56.5\%/\sqrt{N_{\text{trees}}})^2 + (12.0\%)^2} \approx \mathbf{\pm 12.5\%\text{ to } 13.5\%}$. Rather than claiming unscientific zero-error precision, Clover outputs an audit-defensible error bracket accepted by carbon registries.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{\large 6. What Didn't: Failed Experiments \& Honest Disclosures}\\[0.25em]
High-integrity engineering requires openly disclosing failed paths and real-world hurdles:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conservative Default Thresholding (0.25)}: The standard $0.25$ confidence cut-off missed over $50\%$ of suppressed crowns in dense urban thickets (detecting only 104 trees at Jadavpur), grouping multi-stem clusters into single detections.
    \item \textbf{High-Resolution Digital Over-Zoom ($1.75\times$)}: Trying to find small saplings by digitally magnifying imagery caused catastrophic over-segmentation ($539$ crowns on Jadavpur) — the model mistook large primary scaffold branches and fork bifurcations of mature banyans for independent trees.
    \item \textbf{Pond Weeds Counted as Trees (Hydrological False Positives)}: Floating mats of invasive water hyacinth (\textit{Eichhornia crassipes}) in urban lakes shared foliar color with tree crowns, triggering false detections until we added an automated green-water ratio mask.
    \item \textbf{The Ground Census Paywall Barrier}: While \href{https://doi.org/10.36808/if/2019/v145i3/144454}{\textit{Bardhan \& Sarkar (2019)}} verifies Simpson's index $0.91$ and $3.28\,\text{t C/yr}$ for Jadavpur, full-text field stem counts remain behind a paywall; rather than fabricating numbers, we openly document this data boundary.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{\large 7. Known Physical Limitations (What Optical AI Physically Cannot Do)}\\[0.25em]
In accordance with carbon market principles, Clover explicitly discloses four immutable physical limits:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Optical Subcanopy Blindness}: Satellites only photograph the top canopy layer. Suppressed saplings and understory shrubs ($\approx 10\text{--}15\%$ of stand biomass) are completely hidden beneath dominant crowns; true multi-tier vertical strata require airborne laser scanning (3D LiDAR).
    \item \textbf{Closed Canopy Saturation}: In dense tropical rainforests, tree crowns merge into an unbroken green ceiling where individual borders physically do not exist. Individual stem counts break down; dissolved continuous canopy area ($m^2$) is the only valid regulatory metric.
    \item \textbf{The Unavoidable $\pm 12\%$ Regional Error Floor}: Satellite cameras cannot weigh wood. Differences in regional wood density create an unavoidable $\pm 12\%$ error floor that cannot be eliminated without destructive field harvesting (managed in carbon markets via pooled risk buffers).
    \item \textbf{Winter Leaves \& Solar Zenith Shadows}: Bare branches during winter leaf-off periods and long shadows cast by tall buildings obscure leaves and lower detection recall; imagery acquisition must be scheduled during peak regional NDVI greenness.
\end{enumerate}
